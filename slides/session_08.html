The Concept
A prototype is a testable version of your design — before any code is written
Architect analogy: An architect builds a scale model before breaking ground. You prototype before your dev team writes a single line.
🧪
Test Ideas
Validate your concept with real users before committing time and budget to development.
💸
Save Time
Catching a broken flow in a prototype costs minutes. Catching it in code costs days.
💬
Get Feedback
Show, don't tell. A clickable prototype gets far more useful feedback than a static screenshot.
Fidelity
Choose your fidelity based on your goal
Low-fi
Paper sketches or simple digital wireframes. Fast, cheap, and great for early feedback when the concept is still evolving.
Watch out: Hard for users to imagine the final feel.
High-fi
Near-final UI with real colours, fonts, and images. Perfect for user testing when you're validating a specific design direction.
Watch out: Takes longer to build — don't invest too early.
Low-fi = validate structure  ·  High-fi = validate feel

Frame
A screen or page in Figma — the building block of any prototype.
Connection
The arrow you draw between frames — tells Figma where to go next.
Trigger
What starts the interaction — a tap, hover, or drag from the user.
Transition
How the screen moves — slide, fade, push. Controls the feel of the flow.

Best Practices
Prototype Do's and Don'ts
Simple rules that separate productive prototyping from wasted effort.
DO
Test with real users — not just classmates or teammates
Link all key screens so the flow feels complete
Use realistic content — real labels, real data
Test on the actual device (mobile, tablet, desktop)
DON'T
Over-design before you've tested the basic flow
Skip edge cases — users will always find them
Prototype every micro-interaction in early rounds
Present your prototype without context — let users explore on their own first

Step 01 — Create Frames
Build one frame for each screen
In Figma, press F and draw your first frame. Choose a device preset (iPhone 14, Android, Desktop). Repeat for every screen in your flow. Name them clearly: "Splash", "Login", "Home".
Pro tip: Name your frames before linking — it makes the connection arrows readable at a glance.
Step 02 — Connect with Prototype Arrows
Select element → Prototype tab → drag arrow to target
Click the element that should trigger the transition (e.g. a button). Switch to the Prototype tab in the right panel. A blue dot appears — drag it to the destination frame. Set the trigger (On Tap) and transition (Push, Fade, or Slide).
Shortcut: Hold Cmd/Ctrl while dragging to snap to the centre of the target frame.
Step 03 — Present & Test
Click ▶ Present to run your flow
Hit the Play/Present button (top right). Figma opens a preview window that simulates your app. Click through your connections and check that every transition feels right. Share the link with users for remote testing.
Share link: Prototype links let anyone test on their phone without installing anything.
