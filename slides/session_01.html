Concept 01
UI = User Interface
Everything you can see and tap on a screen. Think of UI as the clothes a product wears.
🎨
Visual Design
Colours, fonts, icons, images — what makes an app look good or bad at first glance
🔲
Layout
Where things sit on screen — buttons, cards, menus, images placed in the right spots
👆
Interactive Elements
Buttons, sliders, inputs, checkboxes — anything the user can tap, click or type into
✨
Micro-interactions
Small animations and feedback — like a button changing colour when you press it
Designer_Kid
Concept 02
UX = User Experience
How it feels to use something. You can't see UX — you only notice it when it's missing.
🗺️
Easy to navigate
Can users find what they need without getting lost or confused?
⚡
Fast to complete
Can they finish a task in as few steps as possible — no unnecessary clicks?
😊
Feels good to use
Do users enjoy the experience, or does it frustrate and slow them down?
💡 Simple analogy: A restaurant can look stunning (UI) — but if the service is slow, the menu is confusing, and the waiter is rude — the experience (UX) is terrible. You won't come back.

Concept 03
UI vs UX
UI Designer
Designs how the screen looks
Picks colours, fonts and icons
Creates buttons, cards and forms
Makes it beautiful and consistent
Works in Figma, creating screens
Focuses on: How does it look?
VS
UX Designer
Designs how the product works
Does user research and interviews
Creates wireframes and user flows
Makes it logical and easy to use
Tests with real users, finds problems
Focuses on: How does it feel?

Concept 04
Designer roles in real companies
Same industry, different job titles. Here's how they actually differ.
UI Designer
UI Designer
→ Specialist in visual design
→ Screens, components, style guides
→ Works closely with developers
→ Common in large product teams
UX Designer
UX Designer
→ Specialist in user research
→ Flows, wireframes, testing
→ Works closely with product managers
→ Common in large tech companies
Product Designer
Product Designer
→ Does both UI + UX
→ Thinks about the whole product
→ Owns design from research to launch
→ Most common in startups
🎯 For this course: We learn both UI and UX — making you a well-rounded Product Designer ready for any company size.

Concept 05
Good UX vs Bad UX
Real apps you've used. Look at them through a designer's eyes.
✓ Good UX
Google Maps
One tap to start. Clear turn-by-turn directions. Auto re-routes if you miss a turn. Warns you about traffic before it happens. Zero confusion — it just works.
✗ Bad UX
Typical Bank App
6 screens to check your balance. Password asked twice. Statement is buried under 3 menus. Looks okay — but the experience makes users want to switch banks.
✓ Good UX
Swiggy / Zomato
Find food → pick items → pay → track delivery. The whole order takes under 3 minutes. Every step is obvious. That's intentional UX design working perfectly.
✗ Bad UX
Confusing Checkout Flows
Re-enter your address every order. Login again mid-checkout. 4 confirmation screens. Studies show 70% of users abandon cart because of bad checkout UX.

Hands-on Activity
App UX Audit
Pick 2 apps you use every day. Hunt for good UX and bad UX like a designer would.
Activity
Find Good UX and Bad UX yourself
1
Pick any 2 apps on your phone — Instagram, PhonePe, Amazon, Ola, anything you use often
2
Try to complete one real task in each app — e.g. find a product, send money, book a ride
3
Write down: What felt easy and obvious? What was confusing or took too many steps?
4
Share with the class — 1 thing you liked (good UX) and 1 thing you'd fix (bad UX)
20 min activity  ·  10 min group share  ·  10 min Q&A  ·  No Figma needed today
