Recruiter Mindset
What They Actually Look For
Recruiters spend 3–5 minutes on a portfolio. They're not admiring your UI — they're checking if you can solve problems.

They want to see...

  • ✅ Can you identify a real user problem?
  • ✅ Do you do research before jumping to Figma?
  • ✅ Can you explain why you made each design decision?
  • ✅ Do you iterate based on feedback?
  • ✅ Is your work clearly presented and easy to scan?

They get bored by...

  • ❌ 20 static mockups with no context
  • ❌ "I redesigned Instagram" (with no research)
  • ❌ Projects that just show the final screens
  • ❌ No explanation of what problem was solved
  • ❌ Generic lorem ipsum copy everywhere

Framework
Case Study Structure
01

The Problem

What was broken or missing? Who was affected? One sentence that a 10-year-old could understand.

02

My Role

What did you personally do? Researcher, sole designer, part of a team? Be honest about your contribution.

03

Research

Who did you talk to? What did you learn? Show your interview notes, surveys, or observations — raw proof you understood users.

04

Process

Persona, user flow, wireframes, iterations. Show the messy middle — that's where thinking lives.

05

Final Solution

The polished screens or prototype. Keep it concise — 4–6 key screens max. Label what each one does.

06

Outcome & Reflection

What happened after? What would you do differently? This shows maturity — most beginners skip it.

Writing Tips
Write It Like a Story
📝
Short sentences win
Write like you're explaining to a friend. "I interviewed 5 users and found they hated the checkout flow." Not "Qualitative research methodologies were employed."
🖼
Show, don't just tell
Every claim needs a visual. Saying "I did wireframes" is weak. Showing the actual wireframe is strong. If you don't have it, recreate it.
🔢
Use numbers when you can
"Users completed checkout 40% faster" beats "improved the checkout flow". Even estimated or hypothetical metrics show you think about impact.
🔄
Show the before & after
Side-by-side comparisons of old vs new are incredibly powerful. It makes improvement obvious even to non-designers.

Platforms
Where to Publish
🎨
Behance (Recommended for beginners)
Free, widely recognised by recruiters in India. Easy drag-and-drop case study builder. Good for visual storytelling. Link directly in your resume and LinkedIn.

Notion

Framer / Webflow
Build a real website with your portfolio. Impressive but takes more time. Great once you have 2–3 solid case studies ready.
framer.com / webflow.com

PDF Portfolio
Always have a PDF version for email attachments and walk-in interviews. 8–12 pages, one project per 2 pages. Keep it under 5 MB.

Watch Out
Common Portfolio Mistakes

Too many projects

3 great case studies beats 10 shallow ones. Quality over quantity, always. Recruiters stop reading after the 3rd one.

Only final screens, no process

The most common mistake. Show your research, sketches, wireframes, and iterations. That's your thinking made visible.

Copying popular apps without a brief

"I redesigned Zomato" is only impressive if you explain the problem you were solving and why your solution is better.

No contact information visible

Your email, LinkedIn, and phone should be on your home page and easy to find. Don't make them search for it.

Broken links or empty pages

Test your portfolio on mobile before sending it. A broken link or unresponsive page loses you the interview on the spot.
