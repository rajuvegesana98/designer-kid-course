The Why
Why Mobile-First?
📱
60%+ web traffic is mobile
More than half of all internet browsing happens on a phone. If your design only works on desktop, you're ignoring most users.
🔍
Google ranks mobile-first
Google uses the mobile version of your site for indexing and ranking. A poor mobile UI directly hurts search visibility.
⚡
Forces prioritisation
Small screen = limited space. You're forced to keep only what truly matters — which makes the whole product cleaner.
📈
Better conversion rates
Smooth mobile experiences directly lead to higher sign-ups, purchases, and task completions.
Approaches Compared
Desktop-First vs Mobile-First
Desktop-First
  • Start with a wide, feature-rich layout
  • Then squeeze it into mobile
  • Often leads to hidden menus and tiny text
  • Elements get removed or squished
  • Mobile feels like an afterthought
vs
Mobile-First
  • Start with the smallest screen
  • Add complexity as screen grows
  • Forces clear hierarchy from the start
  • Content is always the priority
  • Desktop becomes an enhancement
Core Concepts
Responsive Design Basics
📐
Breakpoints
Set points where the layout changes. Common: 320px (phone), 768px (tablet), 1024px+ (desktop).
🔲
Fluid Grids
Use percentages or "fr" units instead of fixed pixels. Columns reflow based on available space.
🖼️
Flexible Images
Images should resize within their container. max-width: 100% prevents overflow and distortion.
Real-world analogy: Think of responsive design like a water bottle — the water (content) stays the same but takes the shape of whatever container it's in.
Designing for Thumbs
Touch-Friendly UI Rules
👍
Tap Target Size
Buttons and links should be at least 44×44px. Tiny targets frustrate users and cause mis-taps.
👋
Thumb Zone
The bottom-center of a phone screen is easiest to reach. Put key actions there, not at the top corners.
↕️
Scroll vs Swipe
Vertical scroll is natural on mobile. Horizontal swipe works for carousels. Never mix them unexpectedly.
🚫
Avoid Hover States
Touch screens have no hover. Any info hidden behind hover must be always visible or revealed on tap.
Design Insight
The Thumb Zone
Easy to Reach
  • Bottom navigation bar
  • Floating action button (bottom-right)
  • Search bar at the bottom
  • Primary CTA in thumb zone
✓ WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok all use this
Hard to Reach
  • Top navigation on tall phones
  • Top-right corner icons
  • Small back buttons at top-left
  • Critical actions placed at top
✗ Causes strain, especially on large phones
Hands-On Practice
Activity: Mobile Redesign
What To Do
  1. Pick any website you use regularly on desktop (news, shopping, banking)
  2. Open it on your phone and note 3 things that feel broken or awkward
  3. Sketch a mobile-first version of the homepage — just boxes and labels
  4. Decide: what stays, what moves, what gets cut for mobile?
  5. Share your before/after with the group
✏️ 20 min activity 🗣️ 10 min share ❓ 10 min Q&A
