Visual Hierarchy

Guiding the Eye

Users don't read interfaces — they scan. Hierarchy tells them where to look first, second, and last.

The 4 Levers of Hierarchy
Size — Big = important. Headlines dominate body text.

Colour — High contrast pulls attention first.

Weight — Bold text reads before regular weight.

Position — Top-left is where Western eyes land first.

White Space

Breathing Room Isn't Wasted Space

White space (also called negative space) reduces cognitive load and makes the important elements easier to find.

🫧
Micro Space
Space between letters, lines, and closely related elements. Controls readability.
📦
Macro Space
Space between sections and major page blocks. Creates rhythm and visual flow.
🏷️
Padding vs Margin
Padding is space inside a component. Margin is space outside it. Both matter.
💡 Luxury brands use more white space than discount brands — subconsciously, space signals quality and care.
Alignment

The Invisible Grid

Every element on a screen should visually connect to something else. Misaligned elements create tension and feel unfinished.

⬅️
Left Align
Most natural for text. Easy scanline for the eye.
↔️
Centre Align
Good for hero sections and short, impactful copy.
➡️
Right Align
Useful for numbers in tables and secondary actions.
📏
Grid System
12-column grids keep layouts consistent across breakpoints.
Rule: Never mix alignment styles in the same group of elements. Consistency reads as competence.
Contrast

Make Things Pop

Contrast isn't just about readability — it directs attention and creates visual interest.

Types of Contrast
Colour contrast — Light vs dark, vibrant vs muted

Size contrast — Large headlines vs small body text

Weight contrast — Bold labels vs light descriptions

Shape contrast — Round pill buttons vs sharp rectangular inputs
Low Contrast (Bad)
This text is hard to read on a dark background
High Contrast (Good)
This text is clear and easy to read
Gestalt Principles

How the Brain Groups Things

Gestalt is why we perceive designs as organised wholes — your brain finds patterns automatically.

Proximity — nearby = related
The left cluster reads as a group; the right as a separate one.
Similarity — alike = grouped
Same colour creates columns even though spacing is even.
Closure — gaps get filled
( )
Broken shapes are completed in the mind — logos exploit this.
Continuity — eye follows lines
Progress bars and timelines exploit natural eye-tracking paths.
Balance

Symmetric vs Asymmetric

Symmetric Balance
Even weight on both sides. Feels calm, stable, and formal. Good for corporate and editorial layouts.
Asymmetric Balance
Different weights balanced by contrast and colour. Feels dynamic and modern. Popular in tech and editorial design.
