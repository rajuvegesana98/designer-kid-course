The basics

What is Usability Testing?

You ask real people to try using your design while you watch — silently. Their struggles tell you what to fix.

🧪
The Core Idea
Give a user a task. Watch them do it. Don't help. Note every moment they pause, click wrong, or look confused.
🚫
What it's NOT
It's not asking "do you like it?" Opinions are not the same as behavior. Watching what people do is more honest than what they say.
📍
Real-world example
Zomato watched users struggle to find the "repeat order" button. It was buried in the menu. They moved it front and center — orders went up.
⏱️
When to test
Early (low-fi prototype), mid-project (clickable wireframe), and after launch (live product). Earlier = cheaper to fix.

Two main approaches

Types of Usability Tests

Moderated

You're in the room (or on a call) with the user.

  • ✓Can ask follow-up questions
  • ✓Catch confusion in real time
  • ✓Great for early prototypes
  • ✗Takes more time to schedule
  • ✗Users may feel watched
Best for: complex flows

Unmoderated

User tests on their own, records screen + voice.

  • ✓Fast — many users at once
  • ✓Natural behavior, no pressure
  • ✓Tools: Maze, Lookback, Hotjar
  • ✗Can't ask follow-ups
  • ✗Needs clear task instructions
Best for: quick validation

During the test

What to Look For

You're not grading the user. You're diagnosing the design.

😤
Errors
User clicks the wrong button. Taps back. Tries something that doesn't work. Count how often it happens.
🤔
Hesitation
User pauses before acting. This means the interface isn't clear enough. Where did they stop — and for how long?
🗣️
Think-Aloud
Ask users to say what they're thinking. "I'm looking for the cart… not sure where it is…" — that's gold.
😮
Surprise / Frustration
Body language matters. A sigh, a frown, or a "wait, really?" are all data points worth noting.
✅
Task Completion
Did they finish the task? If yes, how long did it take? A completed task with 10 wrong clicks is still a problem.
🔁
Repeat Errors
If 3+ users make the same mistake in the same place — that's not user error. That's a design problem. Fix it.

After the test

Analyzing Results

You've got notes from 5 sessions. Now turn them into a clear action list.

📋
Step 1 — List All Issues
Write down every problem observed across all sessions. Don't filter yet — just capture everything.
🗂️
Step 2 — Group by Screen
Sort issues by where they happened. Home screen issues, checkout issues, nav issues — grouping reveals hotspots.
📊
Step 3 — Rank by Frequency
Issues that appeared in 4 out of 5 sessions are high priority. One-off issues are low priority. Frequency = severity.
🛠️
Step 4 — Write Fix Notes
For each issue, write a one-line fix. "Move CTA above the fold." "Replace icon with text label." Simple and actionable.

Session Activity

🧪 Usability Test in Pairs
  • 1Pick any app on your phone (e-commerce, food delivery, banking)
  • 2Your partner gives you a task: "Find and add an item to cart without guidance"
  • 3Think aloud while doing it — say everything you're thinking
  • 4Observer writes down: hesitations, errors, facial expressions
  • 5Switch roles — repeat with a different app
  • 6Share 3 usability issues you found with the group
20 min activity 10 min share-out 10 min Q&A
