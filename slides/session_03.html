Concept 01

Who is the user?

🎯
Not "Everyone"
"Everyone" is not a user. A 22-year-old student ordering food is a user. The more specific you get, the better you can design.
👤
A Real Person
Your user has a name, an age, a job, daily habits, frustrations, and goals. Design for that person — not an imaginary average.
🧩
Has Specific Goals
Every user opens your app with a purpose. Find out what that purpose is. That's what your design must serve.
🌍
Lives in a Context
Where are they? On a crowded bus? In a hurry? Using a cheap phone? Context completely changes how design should work.
💡 Designing for "everyone" is like cooking a meal for "anyone". You end up with something that satisfies nobody. Pick your user. Design for them.
Concept 02

Needs vs Wants

What users SAY they want
  • "Make it faster"
  • "Add more features"
  • "Make it look better"
  • "Give me more options"
VS
What users actually NEED
  • "Stop making me repeat steps"
  • "Help me finish this one task easily"
  • "Make the important thing obvious"
  • "Remove the things I never use"
💡 Users describe symptoms, not diagnoses. "Make it faster" usually means "reduce the number of steps". Your job is to find the real need underneath the stated want.
Concept 03

Research Methods

🗣️
User Interviews
Talk to 5–8 real users. Ask open-ended questions. Listen more than you speak. "Tell me about the last time you..." is better than "Do you like...?"
📋
Surveys
Useful for gathering quick opinions from many people. Keep it under 10 questions. Good for confirming things — not for discovering new insights.
👁️
Observation
Watch people actually use a product. Don't ask — observe. What confuses them? Where do they pause? Where do they tap the wrong thing?
🔍
Competitor Research
Study apps that solve the same problem. What do they do well? What do users complain about in reviews? App Store reviews = free user research.
🗒️
Secondary Research
Read existing studies, blog posts and reports about your target users. Someone has already researched your user group — find that research.
Concept 04

How to Run a User Interview

Do this
  • Ask open-ended questions — "Tell me about your experience with..." not "Is this good?"
  • Let them talk — silence is okay. Don't rush to fill it.
  • Ask why, then ask why again — go deeper than the first answer
  • Take notes or record (with permission)
  • Watch their body language — frustration shows before words do
Avoid this
  • Leading questions — "Don't you think this button is confusing?" plants the answer
  • Showing your design too early — let them describe the problem first
  • Asking about the future — "Would you use this?" People are bad at predicting their own behaviour
  • Defending your design — they're not criticising you, they're giving you data
  • Interviewing only friends — friends are polite. You need honest strangers.
Concept 05

Identifying User Pain Points

A pain point is any moment in a user's experience that causes frustration, confusion, or extra effort. Finding them is your biggest job as a UX designer.

😤
Friction Points
Steps that take too long, are confusing, or require too much effort. Example: a 6-step checkout when 2 would do.
🤷
Confusion Points
Moments where users don't know what to do next. Example: a form that gives no feedback when you make an error.
⏱️
Wait Points
Moments where users are forced to wait with no feedback. Example: a loading screen with no progress indicator.
🚫
Dead Ends
When users get stuck with no way forward. Example: an error message that says "Something went wrong" with no action to take.
🎯 You find pain points by watching users struggle — not by asking them what's wrong. "I'm fine" means nothing. "They clicked the wrong button three times" tells you everything.
Activity

Research a Real App

Pick any app you use daily. Become a researcher for 20 minutes.

⚡ App Research Challenge
App Research Challenge
  1. 1Pick one app — something you use often (food delivery, banking, notes, transport)
  2. 2Identify who the main user is — be specific: age, situation, goal
  3. 3Try to complete 2 tasks in the app. Write down every moment of friction
  4. 4Check the App Store reviews — what do real users complain about most?
  5. 5Write 3 pain points you found + suggest what the fix might be
