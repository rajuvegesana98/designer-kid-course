Designer_Kid Studios

Student Problem Statements

20 real-world UX design challenges — one per session. Each problem maps to that session's theory.

How to Use This Document

Each problem statement is handed out at the start of the activity segment. Students solve within the session's 20-minute window. Encourage students to save all deliverables as portfolio artifacts. All problems are intentionally open-ended — there is no single correct answer.

Week 1 — Foundations of UX

S01 — What is UX Design?
Week 1 · Day 1
Context
Your aunt runs a small home-catering business from Bengaluru. She accepts orders through WhatsApp, manually tracks them in a notebook, and often misses orders during peak hours.
Challenge
Define 3 UX problems she faces and propose (in words, no screens needed) what a better experience might look like for her and her customers.
No design tools neededWritten / sketched response20 min
How might we help a solo food entrepreneur manage orders without a technical background?
S02 — User Research Methods
Week 1 · Day 2
Context
A startup is building a mobile app to help college students track their monthly expenses. Before designing anything, they need to understand how students currently manage money.
Challenge
Write a research plan: choose 2 research methods, write 5 interview questions for each, and define what you're trying to learn (your research goals).
Written planNo screens20 min
How might we understand the mental models college students use around spending and saving?
S03 — Empathy Mapping
Week 1 · Day 3
Context
Ravi, 58, is a retired school principal trying to use a government portal to renew his pension documents. He is not confident with technology and often asks his daughter for help.
Challenge
Create an empathy map for Ravi: fill in Says, Thinks, Does, and Feels quadrants. Then write 2 design implications from your map.
Empathy map template (paper)20 min
How might we design government portals that don't require technical confidence to navigate?
S04 — Persona & User Flow
Week 1 · Day 4
Context
A fitness app wants to help first-time gym-goers build a consistent workout habit. They've done research and found their primary user is a 26-year-old software professional who exercises sporadically.
Challenge
Create one persona for this user and draw a user flow for the task: "Set up a weekly workout schedule for the first time."
Persona template (paper or Figma)Flow diagram20 min
How might we help busy professionals commit to a gym routine without feeling overwhelmed?
S05 — IA & Wireframing
Week 1 · Day 5
Context
A community library is building its first website. It needs to serve three types of users: members who want to borrow books online, students who need study room bookings, and the public who want to check upcoming events.
Challenge
Draw a sitemap with 3 levels, then wireframe the homepage — show what the first screen communicates and how each user type can navigate from it.
