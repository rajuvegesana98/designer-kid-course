LinkedIn Profile Guide

Build a profile that gets found by recruiters and tells your design story convincingly — even with zero job experience.

Why LinkedIn Matters for Designers

Recruiters at product companies actively search LinkedIn for junior designers. A well-optimised profile with a portfolio link and the right keywords can generate inbound interview requests before you apply to a single job. Your profile is a permanent design artefact — treat it like your first UX project.

Profile Photo

Photo
  • Use a clean, professional headshot — no group photos, no selfies with distracting backgrounds.
  • Smile, look directly at the camera, and use a plain or soft-focus background.
  • LinkedIn photo dimensions: 400 × 400px minimum, displayed as a circle.
  • Wear what you'd wear to a design team interview.
Profiles with photos get 21× more views and 36× more messages than profiles without one.

Headline

Headline · 220 characters

Your headline is shown in search results. Don't just say "Student" — use keywords recruiters search for.

✅ Strong headlines

  • UI/UX Designer | Figma · User Research · Prototyping
  • Aspiring Product Designer | UX Certified · Portfolio at behance.net/...
  • Junior UI Designer | Mobile-First Design · Design Systems

❌ Weak headlines

  • Looking for opportunities
  • Student at [College Name]
  • Fresher | Available immediately
[Role you're targeting] | [2–3 skill keywords] · [One differentiator or portfolio link]

About / Summary Section

About · 2,600 characters max

Write in first person. Tell your story in 4 short paragraphs: who you are, what you do, what you've built, and how to reach you.

I'm a UI/UX designer focused on [type of problems — e.g., "making complex workflows simple for everyday users"]. I recently completed a 4-week intensive UX course where I designed [number] projects covering research, wireframing, prototyping, and usability testing. My background in [your previous field/education] gives me a unique perspective — [how it connects to design, e.g., "understanding technical constraints helps me design with dev feasibility in mind"]. I'm currently building my portfolio with case studies that cover [mention 1–2 specific projects]. I'm most excited about working on [type of products or problems]. Open to junior designer or internship roles. Connect with me or view my portfolio at [your Behance/portfolio URL].
Include keywords: UX Design, UI Design, Figma, User Research, Wireframing, Prototyping, Usability Testing. LinkedIn's search algorithm indexes the About section.

Experience Section

Experience

If you have no formal UX job experience, list these instead:

  • Freelance projects — even if unpaid, if you designed something for someone, it counts. Describe it with process and outcome.
  • Course project work — list "UI/UX Design Student Project" with the course name, your role, and what you built.
  • Internships or relevant work — any role where you touched design, communication, or problem-solving can be framed to show design thinking.
  • Open-source or community contributions — if you've contributed to a community or non-profit project.
UI/UX Design | Self-directed Project · [Month Year – Month Year] Designed a [type of product — e.g., mobile app for local event discovery] from research to hi-fi prototype. Conducted 5 user interviews, built an empathy map and persona, wireframed 12 screens, and ran 3 rounds of usability testing. Final prototype link: [Figma URL]

Education & Certifications

Education
  • List your degree and any relevant coursework or projects.
  • Add Licences & Certifications for: this Designer_Kid course, Google UX Design Certificate (Coursera), any Figma courses, etc.
  • For certifications, always add the issuing organisation and year.

Skills Section

Skills · Add at least 15

✅ Add these

  • User Interface Design
  • Figma
  • Wireframing
  • Prototyping
  • User Research
  • Usability Testing
  • Design Thinking
  • Information Architecture
  • Design Systems
  • Accessibility

❌ Avoid vague skills

  • Microsoft Office
  • "Communication"
  • "Teamwork"
  • Generic soft skills without design context
Ask 3–5 connections to endorse your top skills. Even endorsements from non-designers carry weight in LinkedIn's algorithm.

Portfolio / Featured Section

Featured
  • Add your Behance, Notion portfolio, or personal site as the top featured item.
  • If you have a PDF case study, upload it as a featured document.
  • You can add up to 4 featured items — use them for your 2–3 best projects and your portfolio link.
  • The featured section appears near the top of your profile — it's the first thing recruiters click.

Networking Strategy

Connect strategically

  • Connect with everyone from this course — your classmates are your future referrals.
  • Send connection requests to designers, product managers, and recruiters at companies you want to work at. Always add a personalised note (100 words max).
  • Follow companies you're interested in to see their job postings and culture content.

Post consistently

  • Post one piece of design work or reflection per week — a wireframe, a before/after, a lesson from this course.
  • Use 3–5 hashtags: #uxdesign #uidesign #figma #designthinking #productdesign
  • Comment on posts from senior designers — thoughtful comments get you noticed faster than most posts.
